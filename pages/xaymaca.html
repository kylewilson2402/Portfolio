Xaymaca

A Dada style collage of Jamaica: ransom note type, torn paper, botanical specimens and the people, music and symbols that make the island what it is.

Xaymaca is the Taíno name for Jamaica, the land of wood and water. This piece is a Dada style collage built out of everything that name holds: history, music, faith, nature and the people who shaped the island.

Bob Marley sits at the centre, framed by a target of green, gold and black and holding an ackee, the national fruit. Around him the island assembles itself out of torn paper: the coat of arms with its motto Out of Many, One People, the doctor bird, lignum vitae and ackee labelled like old botanical specimens, a street scene from King Street in Kingston around 1900, Nanny of the Maroons, Marcus Garvey, Haile Selassie with the Lion of Judah, a sound system stack, a gramophone, old Jamaica stamps and a record spinning at the bottom. Independence day, 6 August 1962, is stamped in on a scrap of tape.

The type is pure Dada: ransom note lettering cut from different sources, XAYMACA and DADA spelled out letter by letter, with patois and Rasta phrases like Wah gwaan, irie, Jah and One Love dropped in like found text. Rough torn edges, halftone photos and specimen captions keep it feeling handmade and loud, the way a Dada poster should.
